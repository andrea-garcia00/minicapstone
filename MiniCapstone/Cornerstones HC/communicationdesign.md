A large body of research has documented many aspects of human perception and cognition, resulting in a set of principles that captures how humans organize and interpret what they see and hear. Illustrations—in published works as well as presentations and video—will communicate effectively only if they respect these principles. Similarly, objects that invite specific uses must be designed to communicate those uses clearly.

**Big Question:** How do we interpret artistic communication?

You attend a talk about conscious spending where the presenter uses a powerpoint presentation to complement his speech. You notice that his slides have very different fonts and jarringly contrasting colors, which confuses you because they talk about the same topic. You also find it hard to understand what is important in each slide because their texts are dense and font size small. The diagrams don’t display any of the information on the slides, but rather data that has to do with another part of the speech, making it hard to follow. You realize that his communications were ineffective and you are left confused by the end of their talk because their visual communications didn’t adhere to any of the cognitive principles of communication design.

***Footnote:*** *There is too much information being displayed, violating the principle of limited capacity (states that humans have limited cognitive capacity for understanding information). The diagrams and numbers don’t complement the text, which violates the principle of compatibility and the lack of consistency and change in color/font does not indicate a change in content as humans normally expect, which violates the principle of informative change, rendering communications ineffective.*

How can we distinguish between similar communication principles?

- Have you adhered to the main principles of communication design?

- Do your visuals enhance the communication, providing a value add to your work?

- Have you explained how using #communicationdesign improved your ability to communicate effectively?

- Are all graphics used clear and relevant to your message?

- Are slide template or theme choices justified with explanation about how they aid your purpose?

- One or more of the main principles of communication design have been neglected without explanation.

- Visuals do not distract from the communication, but they do not add anything either.

- Visuals are blurry, unrelated, or watermarked.

- A slide template was applied without critical analysis on its effectiveness for your purpose.

#### Applying the HC at Minerva

Anytime you are creating a slideshow or presentation that includes bullets, graphics, or visuals, this HC will help you communicate your main ideas effectively. By utilizing communication design principles, you can keep your audience engaged.

Some examples of applications include:

- In the natural science college, you can use #communicationdesign to explain your research findings to others through visuals and graphics. By using the principles of #communicationdesign you can hold your audience’s attention while adding to their understanding of your findings. For example, explaining how research in clean energy has expanded and impacted climate change since 2000 could be aided by incorporating important visuals, graphics, and timelines that adhere to #communicationdesign.

- In the social science college, #communicationdesign could help you build slides to pitch your social-action idea to funders. By having visuals that will enhance your communication by illustrating the social issue and possible impact of your idea, you can capture your audience and ensure your idea becomes change.

- When designing social media posts for your small business, #communicationdesign can be the difference between an active following and a failure to reach you audience. On social media these principles are particularly important because the audience tends to be impatient and disengaged. Therefore, stunning visuals with short but detailed bullets can draw possible customers in and help your business grow!

- As with all HCs, the applicability of communication design principles may vary depending on #audience ([[audience]]). The principles are useful and can open your eyes to communication nuances, but they are not universal and timeless. Adjust applications thoughtfully for different audience expectations. This could look like violating a principle on purpose to convey a particular meaning (for example, purposely cluttering a slide with content to convey a sense of “chaos” to make a salient point for the audience). Strong applications might also consider different perceptions across cultures. For example, some Asian countries prefer bright, information-dense slides, reflecting a holistic way of thinking and the importance of context, rather than the principles of limited capacity. In South Korea, presentations often include numerous facts, figures, and visuals, with overloaded slides delivered at a fast pace. Visit these links to learn more about design across different regions and presentations in South Korea. Even color can symbolize different things across different cultures. Blue is believed to be the safest and most commonly positively perceived color across cultures, although it could also stand for sadness. If this interests you, visit this article to learn about tips for presentations across cultures. These are just a couple of examples that illustrate how the applicability of the communication design principles can vary based on your #audience.

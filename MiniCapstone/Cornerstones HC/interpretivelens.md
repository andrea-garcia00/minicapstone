## #interpretivelens

### Be mindful of how prior experiences, expectations, and judgments affect inferences drawn from different forms of communication and react accordingly.

**Please fill out this Google Form to submit suggestions for this page (i.e. typos, study guide contributions, useful applications, etc.). We are actively seeking feedback on anything big or small to make the HC Handbook as useful as possible.**

We can only appreciate the perspectives of ourselves and others when we understand how our past shapes our viewpoints.

Being open-minded requires being receptive to new ideas, even when they might conflict with your beliefs, worldview, or assumptions. We need to learn to recognize the effects of our prior experiences and expectations, and try to see the same material from different perspectives. One should consider relevant evidence and arguments and be receptive to new ideas and alternative solutions. The information in messages is not always what one expects or hopes to receive and thus one must carefully consider what actually is being communicated—not just what one anticipates.

Your teacher assigns a paper on impressionist art. You go to a museum with the intention of observing a Van Gogh painting and critiquing it for the purposes of the assignment. You have never seen impressionist art before, but you have a friend who loves it--unfortunately, that friend and you are in a slight argument, and you recognize that those negative emotions are affecting the way you view the painting. You aren’t able to pay close attention to its compositional features because you cannot take your mind off of your friend and the difficulty you two are having lately--the reds in the painting only make you think of anger, and the blues make you think of the melancholy feelings you are currently harboring towards your friendship. When writing your paper, you acknowledge the first impression you had of the painting, but choose to expand your analysis by coming back on a different day when you are feeling better. This allows you to discuss the differences in interpretation and extract richer conclusions about how impressionist techniques communicate feelings.

***Footnote:*** *A personal experience that had the potential to affect the interpretation of the painting was identified, and you ensured that your understanding of--and proper critique of--the work would not be hindered by this experience/negative emotion.*

- In every class, you will interpret your classmates’ and professor’s ideas and communicate your thoughts. Your individual experiences, expectations, and purposes will shape this communication, and you must consciously work to recognize when a disagreement arises from different #interpretivelens. People often express themselves through different words, gestures, and examples but mean the same thing. Determining when your #interpretivelens is inhibiting your understanding and actively striving for an open mind and broader #interpretivelens will elevate your classroom experience

- Before coming to Minerva, we all received education on varying topics, and some parts of this education were particularly impactful. For example, imagine that you took a Latin Philosophy class that changed your perspective on feelings. In this class, you discussed how society labels certain feelings as “good” and others as “bad,” which creates the idea that “happiness” is the absence of negative feelings. “Happiness,” when defined this way, is an unachievable goal that people obsess over nonetheless. As a result, people are afraid to feel negative emotions and avoid them by dangerous and unhealthy means rather than letting themselves feel how their body needs them. Since this was particularly impactful, you might draw from these ideas in a creative assignment where you design a dystopian world. In this world, you depict how the seemingly utopian vision of a town that embodies modern “happiness” is not a utopia at all. You then tag #interpretivelens and explain how that class changed how you thought about happiness and how this changed perception influenced the world you created.

- You will use this HC in your everyday life and relationships. So often, your interpretation of interactions will differ from those around you based on your unique #interpretivelens. Even though you can not understand everyone’s perspective, it is helpful to acknowledge that views differ and avoid assuming that your feelings are universal.

- Your #interpretivelens plays a role in social media too. Your Instagram algorithm is likely based on your unique #interpretivelens, constantly feeding you products you like and information you already agree with. This algorithmic optimization to your interests is problematic because it works to narrow your #interpretivelens and satisfy your confirmation bias. If you are working toward a more open mind, you must actively look for information you disagree with or would not otherwise be exposed to. This way, you can use social media like Instagram to widen your #interpretivelens rather than inhibit it.

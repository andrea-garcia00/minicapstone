## #expression

### Interpret, evaluate, and utilize nonverbal communication.

**Please fill out this Google Form to submit suggestions for this page (i.e. typos, study guide contributions, useful applications, etc.). We are actively seeking feedback on anything big or small to make the HC Handbook as useful as possible.**

Your face says it all, so make sure it says what you intend it to.

Nonverbal cues are a critical part of communication. Our bodies’ positioning, gestures, and movement can reinforce what we are communicating verbally or undermine our meaning. In particular, facial expressions convey enormous amounts of information, ranging from emotion to intention. Likewise, verbal delivery, intonation, and pacing strongly impact how a message is received. However, we must understand that although some aspects of body language, facial expression, and intonation are common across cultures, other aspects are culturally constructed—which helps us more accurately read the expressions of others and make our own communication more effective. Body language, facial expression, and intonation are a key component of communication, so one should learn to use them effectively.

- In class, #expression is significant. In small classes, everyone can see the faces of their peers, and each person's expression seriously impacts the class environment. Showing up with an enthusiastic expression and mindset lets you get the most out of class. Facial expressions can encourage peers to help promote positive discussions or undermine discussions by conveying a lack of interest in what others say. Remember that you’re constantly communicating when you are on camera – You can improve your application of #expression by doing so thoughtfully and with purpose.

- In a recorded presentation, you should always be aware of your expression. When presentations are recorded, every little expression can be rerun and analyzed. Looking engaged is essential, not just for the speaker's benefit but also to maximize your time and learn thoroughly. The pace of your speech and intonation are crucial to consider when trying to convey your ideas clearly and persuasively. Strong applications of #expression typically involve recording drafts of your work, watching them and reviewing them for elements of expressive communication (possibly with a peer), and then re-recording them to reflect improvements.

- In different concentrations, norms of #expression may differ. This tends to result from field-specific #professionalism norms, and you can thoughtfully apply these two HCs in tandem if you carefully explain how the choices you make address the priorities of both HCs. For example, for a business pitch, you may express yourself through animated facial expressions and large hand gestures as a #expression choice that you justify through thoughtful application of #professionalism, based on research you conduct by watching other business pitches that are deemed to be highly effective in their professional context.

- For many people, clothes are a form of expression. Fashion is an art form as well as a daily practice. When thinking of fashion as an application of #expression, it is essential to distinguish the application from #medium. There will likely be some overlap between applications. However, if #expression is the focus, the clothes should be seen through embodied communication. Ask yourself how this piece interacts with my body language to convey an emotion or vibe – we often find it fun to express ourselves through style!

- When caring for pets, we commonly rely on facial expressions, body language, and tone of voice to convey positive and corrective messages. Through observation, education, and trial and error, we can learn to improve our communication across species to help connect more effectively with pets and other animals we encounter.

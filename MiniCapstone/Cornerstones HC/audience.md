## #audience

### Tailor oral and written work by considering the situation and perspective of the people receiving it.

**Please fill out this Google Form to submit suggestions for this page (i.e. typos, study guide contributions, useful applications, etc.). We are actively seeking feedback on anything big or small to make the HC Handbook as useful as possible.**

Everyone can have brilliant ideas; successfully sharing these ideas with others is the challenge.

Different audiences have different background knowledge, interests, goals, world-views and perspectives. To apply #audience effectively, one must do two crucial steps: 1) characterize: identify and articulate characteristics of an audience and 2) tailor: adjust details and features of a communication to match and speak to these characteristics. Applications of audience become especially nontrivial when an audience consists of people who have different levels of knowledge, goals, interests, or motivations; in these cases, one cannot reach all of them equally effectively with the exact same delivery. Learning about and carefully considering an audience’s point of view can help one design communications that they can grasp, will appeal to their interests, and will be perceived as compelling.

**Big Question:** What might a perfect society look like?

You present a new product to a company that you hope will buy it in three independent meetings with the directors, engineers, and sales team. Your first meeting is with the directors. In your experience, directors are not interested in the technical details of the product, because they are more focused on making sure the product is a worthy investment. Thus, you presented a 5-minute summary of its functionality, potential revenue and time to profitability. Next, you present it to the engineers, who want an in-depth description of its “specs” and the potential problems during manufacturing, since their job relies on the product’s mechanical aspects. You focus on the technical aspects of the product, taking no time to explain technical jargon knowing your audience is knowledgeable in the field. Finally, you present it to the sales team, and they ask about the most novel and noteworthy features of the product that they can use to advertise it to customers. You give them a description of the product’s most useful functions and explain its most sensational aspects. You adjusted both the content and the manner in which you present it to each audience—including the length of your presentation—to meet the audience’s expectations and interest.

***Footnote:*** *The content and method of the presentation are adapted to suit each specialized target audience based on an understanding of their main interests and perspectives. For example, for the directors, who are less interested in the product details and who have time constraints, the presentation was made to be short and to focus on their area of interest: profitability.*

#### Applying the HC at Minerva

You will apply this HC in most assignments. Even when you do not tag this HC for grading, it will benefit you to consider your intended audience and tailor your message to them. Communication is inherently personal, and knowing how to reach people with your message consistently improves communication.

Some examples of applications include:

- When solving problem sets in a computer science class, you imagine your audience is your peers. By defining your audience in this way, you can ensure your explanation is appropriately detailed and explained. This is preferable to assuming the audience is your professor with advanced knowledge, leaving out important details, or assuming your audience knows nothing and wasting time explaining unimportant steps.

- When writing a paper about how your culture's norms manifest in your country's politics, you should define your audience. If talking to people from your culture, your audience's background knowledge will allow you to talk about niche examples and save you extensive explanations. On the other hand, if your audience is your Minerva peers, you will have to tailor your message to a group that shares very little background knowledge about your culture but has many interests and education in common. In this case, you will have to do more explaining about your culture but will be able to utilize shared knowledge on complex systems in your paper.

- When presenting in other states or cities, a political candidate gives different speeches to align her message with the diverse backgrounds and interests of people from different regions.

- A taxi driver knows from experience that when a group of people enters his car, they tend to talk amongst themselves and prefer he stays out of the conversation. On the other hand, individual passengers tend to enjoy some conversation. He has also noticed his older passengers are usually more talkative than his younger passengers. Based on his observations, he decides whether to engage in conversation based on his passengers' quantity and age.

- A museum curator changes how they phrase a particularly controversial figure depending on whether or not the prospective investors agree with the figure's message.

- A TV channel considers when to program certain shows, given who will likely tune in. Therefore, shows meant for adults and parents are streamed in the evening when most adults are done with work and their children are asleep.

- In making an app, a user experience (UX) designer might use #audience and #designthinking ([[designthinking]]) to gather product feedback. They might use #audience to a) identify who they want to interview and b) form the questions they will ask them in the interview.

- When writing to parents at home, you are most likely applying to tailor your message to what your parents want to hear. You may prioritize writing about your academic accomplishments and write less about your social life and how much money you are spending.

## #gapanalysis

### Identify and evaluate whether there are suitable existing solutions to a problem or whether a creative new solution is required.

**Please fill out this Google Form to submit suggestions for this page (i.e. typos, study guide contributions, useful applications, etc.). We are actively seeking feedback on anything big or small to make the HC Handbook as useful as possible.**

When it comes to solutions, there's no need to reinvent the wheel (unless there is).

A novel solution to a problem is only required when an adequate solution does not already exist. Thus, the first step in “filling a gap” with a new product, process, or procedure is determining whether there is a gap to be filled. This requires evaluating whether existing solutions are sufficient, can be suitably modified, or if a new solution is needed instead.

**Unit:** Problem Solving

**Big Question:** How can we feed the world?

You work for a bakery and discover that the amount of flour you order is not being adjusted based on how much was sold in the last few days and seasonal specialties, resulting in wastage. You aim to waste less than 5% of the flour you order and have sufficient surplus so that if there is an unexpected surge in business you can keep up. You consider managing the amount of flour in a spreadsheet, given this is what most other bakeries do, but find it takes too much manual labor. While the other bakeries have more employees to handle that work, you are short on staff and would not be able to do the same. You check other order-management software but find their features are not suitable for small-scale bakeries like yours. Having failed to find any suitable software that could estimate the needed amount and order it automatically, you hire your neighbor’s daughter to write some computer code to help optimize ordering. Upon using the code to determine how much flour to order, you notice you are only wasting 4.5% of the flour ordered.

***Footnote:*** *A thorough consideration of potential existing solutions is carried out. After concluding that existing solutions fall short, a new solution is developed to fill this “gap” in solutions, benefiting from highlighting unique features currently unavailable in alternative solutions.*

Is #gapanalysis the right HC? If the answer to the following questions is yes, #gapanalysis could be useful:

1. Are you analyzing or modifying existing solutions, rather than creating novel solutions?
2. Are you critiquing the strengths and weaknesses of current solutions?

Depending on the focus of the work, there might be other applicable HCs to consider. Consult the following table listing possible related HCs.

The following HC might apply if the work:

- Analyzes *constraints* (non-negotiable restrictions), instead of existing solutions’ shortcomings.

- Uses frameworks to create *new solutions*, rather than evaluating or modifying existing solutions.

- Draws solutions from problems that are similar in structure but in a different domain, followed by similarity mapping and modifications tailored to the target problem’s context. Drawing the exact solution from another problem is an application of #gapanalysis (implying there is no need to devise novel solutions).

- Identifies the initial state, goal state, and key obstacles of a *problem*. Note that any gap between the initial and goal state is the *problem* and thus #rightproblem instead of #gapanalysis.

- Performs a SWOT analysis (strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, threats) on an *organization*.

- The application misidentifies the “gap” between the initial and final states of a problem as a gap analysis.

- The application lists a few existing solutions but does not evaluate their potential in depth.

- The application overlooks a particularly obvious and useful existing solution (e.g., not a thorough enough search for workable existing solutions)

- The application only explores one existing solution.

- The application creates a novel solution but misidentifies it as part of a gap analysis.

- There is misalignment between the goal state and the gap analysis.

- For Cornerstones Civic Projects, one of the first steps is to perform research on the topic. This helps students acquire background knowledge, learn the current status of the issue, and refine their project direction. This usually involves evaluating existing solutions to the challenge problem and identifying possible gaps in the current solutions. Students might then propose to fill one or more gaps as their final deliverable.

  - For example, one CCP group was working on designing optimal hybrid spaces to foster equity in educational experiences. They identified the existence of hybrid learning programs that they could refer to, such as the ZoomRoom and the Learning Studio. After the initial gap analysis, they noted that the mere existence of a hybrid learning room was not a suitable reference point for their deliverable idea. They carefully considered the constraints that an optimal solution should include, such as a guide that can aid the design process through questions. Upon further landscape analysis, they found that Steel Case proposed an adjacent solution worth considering. The advantages of the Steel Cases design were identified. However, the group decided there was still a solution gap to fill as the Steel Case solution had a key disadvantage. They took note of this disadvantage and aligned their solution towards filling that missing part.

- #gapanalysis is a core HC in Empirical Analyses.

  - As an example, a student was working to solve the subproblem of drug addiction in the Tenderloin area of San Francisco and completed a gap analysis. They first identified the key constraints related to budget and the chronic availability of drugs on the streets. Since homeless people have low incomes, the analysis assumed that the non-profit would have to provide drug treatment services for free. They also assumed that illegal drugs would always be available on the streets.
  - They then explored existing solutions such as pharmacotherapy, therapeutic community, and outpatient counseling. For reasons including the lack of a current medicine for methamphetamine addiction and cost considerations, none of the existing solutions were considered workable. They thus used evidence to make the case that a novel solution would be required.

- The Gap Analysis Project (2019) aims to study the efficiency of nature conservation efforts by collecting species, land cover, and protected areas data. Using the dataset, they evaluate current strategies and determine if new strategies are needed. For example, the project found out that most protection was allocated to regions that lack biodiversity, while biodiversity-rich regions were neglected. The data helped decision makers to reform land protection programs (the existing solutions). Refer to the official website for more information.
